chapter six

Kitchen survival guide

Recipe #1: Crispy pierogies

Store-bought frozen pierogies, soft inside, crispy outside. Serves 2.

  1. Boil a pot of salted water. Drop in about 12 frozen pierogies and stir once so they don't stick.
  2. When they float, give them 1–2 more minutes (about 5 minutes total). Drain and let them dry for a minute. Wet pierogies won't crisp.
  3. Meanwhile, slice an onion and cook it in a pan with 2 tbsp butter and a splash of oil, medium heat, 8–10 minutes until golden. Push it to the side.
  4. Add the pierogies in one layer. Don't crowd the pan. 3–4 minutes per side, without poking them, until golden and crispy.
  5. Serve with the onions, sour cream and a pinch of salt. Bacon bits if you're feeling fancy.

No-boil shortcut: fry them frozen in oil for 2 minutes, add ¼ cup water, cover for 5 minutes, then uncover and let them crisp up.

Recipe #2: Pasta bolognese

The real-deal meat sauce with store-bought pasta. Serves 4, or 2 plus lunch tomorrow.

  1. Finely chop 1 onion, 1 carrot, 1 celery stalk and 2 garlic cloves. Cook them in 2 tbsp olive oil over medium heat for 8–10 minutes until soft.
  2. Turn the heat up, add 500 g ground beef (or half beef, half pork) with salt and pepper. Break it up and let it brown properly, about 8 minutes.
  3. Stir in 2 tbsp tomato paste for a minute, then ½ cup red wine if you have it and let it bubble away.
  4. Add a 796 ml can of crushed tomatoes, a bay leaf and 1 tsp dried oregano. Simmer on low, lid half on, for at least 30 minutes (an hour is better), stirring now and then.
  5. Finish with a splash of milk or cream, then taste and add salt.
  6. Cook 400 g spaghetti, tagliatelle or rigatoni in very salty water, 1 minute less than the package says. Save a cup of pasta water.
  7. Toss the pasta into the sauce with a splash of pasta water until glossy. Top with Parmesan.
If the smoke alarm joins in, you've gone too far. Stay by the stove, Matt.
